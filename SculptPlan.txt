HCP65-Sculpt: the plan
======================
A program for looking at and changing procedural materials on a shape: a 3D view over a node editor, as Blender's Shading
workspace.  It began as "a textures tab" at the end of the texture generators' plan
(CommonLibraries/Libraries/ProceduralLib/TexturePlan.txt, whose steps 1 to 38 made what it shows) and was moved here
2026-11-17.  Built, steps 1 to 24, the same day: each step has a note of what was done and where that differs from what
was proposed, and Questions for the user (near the end) lists what's the user's to answer or to know.


The user's decisions (2026-11-17)
  Looking at textures is not a tab in WindowInputTester.  It's its own project, HCP65-Sculpt, in its own repository
  (Codaphela/HCP65-Sculpt, github andrewpaterson/HCP65-Sculpt; licence files only so far), made with WindowInputTester
  as its inspiration.
  The isosurface tab comes out of WindowInputTester.
  The view both programs draw with goes in a new library in CommonLibraries, SceneViewLib.
  SceneViewLib holds CSceneCanvasDraw; CSceneView, a canvas with the view's mouse and keys and a hook for a click that
  wasn't a drag, which the tester's scene tab and Sculpt both use; and the turntable (target, yaw, pitch, distance,
  framing a scene's bounds, ScreenToPlane) as a plain struct apart from the drawing, so TestSceneViewLib can test it
  without a window.  Not in it: the VRML view (the tester's alone) and the pages.
  The material's graph is edited in a node editor, as Blender's.
  A decision is taken from what Blender does wherever that is unambiguous, and only asked otherwise.  (The user knows
  3D Studio Max, not Blender; Blender is followed as the more used now.)
  No C++ is written out to paste in.  A material is a configuration, data that says what to call with what settings,
  which may be written to disk.  SolidMaterials stays as a help to programmers: functions that make a configuration.
  The configuration is per node: the graph itself, each node naming its operator and holding its settings (not the
  name of a Make function with a seed).

What it's for: the materials' colours and sizes are first guesses, and changing one means changing SolidMaterials.cpp,
building and running the tests for the gallery.  Sculpt is a shape and a material on it, the graph and its numbers
changed and seen at once.  It's where the isosurface tab's dropping of spheres goes too.

The repository, as HCP65-IDE's is laid out:
  HCP65-Sculpt/HCP65Sculpt/CMakeLists.txt, HCP65Sculpt.sln      The Linux build and the solution.
  HCP65-Sculpt/HCP65Sculpt/HCP65Sculpt/                         The sources, HCP65Sculpt.vcxproj (CMake reads it), Resources.
  HCP65-Sculpt/Libraries, Tests                                 When something wants testing apart from the window.
  LICENSE, COPYING, .gitattributes, .gitignore                  There (d06e111).
  CommonLibraries/repos.py's REPOS has HCP65-Sculpt.

What comes from WindowInputTester, and how.  Two repositories can't share a source file, so what both want goes to
CommonLibraries and the rest is written again, smaller:
  CSceneCanvasDraw                                              To SceneViewLib (above).
  The app's start (WinMain, the X11 main, the app, its tick, the font)     Written again in Sculpt from the tester's,
      without the tester's pages: some 500 lines.
  CIsosurfacePage                                               Moves to Sculpt and becomes its page.

Taken from Blender (its Shading workspace and Shader Editor; each is what Blender does, so not asked)
  The window        The 3D view above, the node editor below, a split between; a menu bar on top.
  The 3D view       The middle button's drag turns the view, with Shift it pans, the wheel zooms, Home frames
                    everything.  The left button is for the thing being worked on (here, dropping a sphere), not for
                    the view.  CSceneView's buttons are therefore told to it: the tester's scene tab keeps its own.
  The preview       A material is looked at on a simple shape chosen from a few: sphere, box (Blender's cube), and
                    ours besides: the two met, and what's been sculpted.
  A node            A box with its name in a header, its output socket on the right at the top, its settings in the
                    middle and its input sockets down the left.  Sockets are coloured by what they carry.
  A link            A curve from an output to an input, made by dragging from one socket to the other.  An output
                    feeds any number of inputs; an input takes one link, and a new one replaces it.  Dragging a link
                    off an input and letting go over nothing removes it.
  An input with no link   Uses a value of its own, shown on the node beside the socket as a field (a colour or a
                    number) that goes away when a link is made.
  Every setting shown     All of a node's fields are on the node.  (Blender's way of showing a few named settings is
                    a group of nodes with chosen inputs: later, if wanted.)
  The output        One node, Material Output, is what the shape is given: the graph's top is whatever is linked to
                    it.  Nodes not linked through to it are kept and do nothing.
  Adding            Shift+A, or the Add menu, opens a menu of nodes by kind (Texture, Color, Converter, Vector); the
                    new node is under the pointer.
  Selecting, moving, removing   Click selects, Shift+click adds to the selection, a drag on the background selects in
                    a box, a drag on a node moves the selected ones.  X or Delete removes them, Shift+D copies them.
  Getting about     The middle button's drag pans the editor, the wheel zooms, Home frames all the nodes.
  A number          A field dragged sideways to change it, clicked to type in it; one with a range (0 to 1) shows how
                    far along it is as a bar.
  A ramp            Blender's Color Ramp: a bar of the ramp's colours with its stops as markers to drag, + and - to add
                    and remove a stop, and the chosen stop's place and colour under it.
  A colour          A swatch; clicking it opens a picker with red, green, blue; hue, saturation, value; and hex.
                    Blender's picker also has a wheel: left until wanted.
  Undo              Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, over every change to the graph.
  Names             A node is titled as Blender's where it has the same thing: Noise Texture, Voronoi Texture
                    (CSolidCells), Gradient Texture, Brick Texture, Color Ramp, Mix (CSolidMerge and CSolidMask),
                    Hue/Saturation/Value and Brightness/Contrast (CSolidAdjust).  The classes keep their names.
  Saving            Everything in one file: the shape, the graph, where its nodes are, the view.  File's New, Open,
                    Save, Save As.

Where ours differs from Blender, and what's done about it
  One socket, not several.  Blender's link carries one thing (a colour, a number, a vector) and a texture node has a
  socket for each.  Ours carries a whole sample (colour, height, shine, gloss, glow), and an operator reads the lot.  So
  a node has one output and its links are one colour.  Taking one channel out (Blender's Separate) or putting channels
  from different textures together (Combine) needs two small operators, CSolidChannel and CSolidCombine: step 17.
  Baked, not drawn live.  Blender works a procedural texture out for every pixel on the graphics card as the view is
  drawn.  Ours is baked into the mesh's texels on the processor: half a second to a second for the sphere from the
  texture plan's step 31.  Proposed: on a change bake at a quarter of the density (a sixteenth of the texels, some 50 ms) at once,
  and at the whole density when nothing has changed for a third of a second.  Measured before it's built (step 12).
  An operator's input can be nothing.  The operators were written to be given their inputs.  With links that can be
  taken off, each must do something sensible with none: the input's own value, as Blender's (step 8).
  Where a node is.  CSolidTexture is SupportLib's and knows nothing of editors.  Sculpt keeps a CSculptNode for each
  texture (the texture, where its box is), and saves those.
  Settings come from Class().  Every operator's Class() already names its fields and their kinds (that's how they
  save), so a node's rows are made from it and a new operator needs nothing written in Sculpt.  Two things Class()
  doesn't say are told in a small table in Sculpt, by class and field: which integers are enums (and their names), and
  which numbers have a range.

Where the node editor's code goes.  Proposed: the boxes, sockets, links, selecting, moving, panning and zooming as a
component in WindowLib (CNodeEditor) that knows nothing of textures: it's asked of through an interface (what nodes,
what sockets, may this link be made) which Sculpt implements over the solid textures.  Logicim's circuit editor is the
nearest thing there is (LogicimLib, on SupportLib's CGeometryMap): looked at first, for what can be shared.
The draggable number (CNumberField), the ramp (CColourRampBar) and the colour picker go in WindowLib too.

Steps
The project
1. SceneViewLib in CommonLibraries: CSceneCanvasDraw moved from WindowInputTester (vcxproj, the tester's CMake and
   solution); the tester builds and runs as it did.  Then the turntable split out as a struct, CSceneView made (its
   buttons told to it) and the tester's scene tab put on it, and TestSceneViewLib (the turntable, ScreenToPlane, a
   small scene drawn by the rasteriser; Vulkan needs a device and isn't tested).
   Done.  STurntable (Turntable.h) is the view's maths; CSceneCanvasDraw keeps two, where the view started and where it
   is.  CSceneView's left button is only held back for a click when something is told of clicks, so the tester's scene
   tab turns at once as it did.  CSceneCanvasDraw gained UseVulkan (false draws with the rasteriser and never looks
   for a device: the tests) and GetImage (what it last drew).  Found on the way: CInstanceMesh::Pose gave the positions
   of the editor's mesh before its modifiers, so a mesh made by modifiers had no bounds and was framed as if at the
   origin; it poses the editor's output now, as it's drawn.  TestSceneViewLib: the turntable, and a box in a window
   with no screen drawn by the rasteriser, zoomed, put back and clicked.
2. HCP65-Sculpt's project: the solution, the project, CMake; an app that opens an empty window with a menu (File:
   Exit).
   Done.  CSculptApp (the window, the menu, the input) and three mains: Windows', X11's and HCP65SculptRef's, which
   draws the window in memory with no screen (WinRefLib).  With HCP65_SCULPT_SNAPSHOT=file the window is saved as a PNG
   once drawn, and HCP65_SCULPT_EXIT then closes it: that's how it's looked at as it's built.  The font is read by
   SupportLib's new ReadTrueTypeFont, which the tester uses too; the IDE, LogiCim and the emulator still have their
   own copies of it (GenerateFont), left alone.  repos.py has HCP65-Sculpt already.
3. CIsosurfacePage moved to Sculpt as its page (CSculptPage) with Blender's buttons: spheres dropped and baked with
   the checker, as now.
   Done.  The page is SculptLib's (HCP65-Sculpt/Libraries/SculptLib), not the app's, so it's tested without the app:
   TestSculptLib (HCP65-Sculpt/Tests) shows it in a window with no screen, drops a sphere, clicks and clears.  Its
   SculptTestWindow makes that window with Sculpt's font and theme for the tests to come.  The view's status no
   longer names keys (they were the tester's scene tab's, which says them itself now), and says playing or paused
   only of a scene with animations.
4. The isosurface out of WindowInputTester: the page, File's Isosurface, ProceduralLib from its CMake, project and
   solution.
   Done.
The node editor (WindowLib, with tests at each step)
5. CNumberField: dragged, typed in, a range's bar, a bad number put back.
   Done (WindowLib's NumberField.h).  The name is on the left and the number on the right of a bar (the progress
   bar's draw); a drag is measured from where the button went down, so dragging back puts the number back; a click
   swaps the bar for a text field (CNumberFieldEdit, as the table's cell editor is made).  A field must be able to
   take the focus to be dragged: a drag is the focussed component's.
6. CNodeEditor: nodes as boxes with a header and sockets from the interface; pan, zoom, Home.
7. Links drawn; selecting, box selecting, moving; making, replacing and removing links by dragging.
   Done, with step 6 (WindowLib's NodeEditor.h and NodeBox.h).  How it is, where that differs from what was proposed:
   The editor isn't asked of through an interface for what nodes there are.  It holds its nodes and links itself, as
   a tree holds its rows: whoever's nodes they are adds a CNodeBox for each (a title, outputs, settings, inputs,
   added top to bottom) and the links between them, and is asked and told of what the pointer and keys do through a
   CNodeEditorListener (may this link be made, a link made or removed, nodes moved, delete, duplicate, add).
   A node is real components: its settings are number fields and whatever else, which take the pointer themselves.
   An input's value (a number field, say) shows in place of its name until something is linked to it.
   A link's curve is the diff divider's, now SupportLib's VectorLevelCubic, which both call; a link is that curve
   stroked as short round-ended pieces (CImageDraw's DrawStroke), its controls pushed out at least 40 pixels so a link
   going back on itself loops round.  The divider and the editor's and nodes' backs all paint through a new base,
   CCelCanvasDraw (a cel kept until what's painted changes).
   Not done: zoom.  A node's settings are real components, and WindowLib has no way to draw a component at
   another size (question 2).  Pan (the middle button's drag) and Home are done.  Shift can't be pressed in a test
   (there's no keyboard in one), so adding to the selection with it is untested.
The graph in Sculpt
8. Operators with an input missing: each input's own value (ProceduralLib; tests).
   Done, by finding there was nothing to add to the operators: each already gives something with an input missing
   (opaque black; a warp with nothing to warp by doesn't move its input; a cavity with no shape is a flat surface's
   grey), which TestSolidOperatorsUnlinked now holds them all to.  An input's own value isn't a new field on every
   operator: it's a flat texture (CSolidFlat) linked to the input, which Sculpt shows as the input's value on the node
   and not as a node.  A saved material then has nothing in it but textures, and a program reading one needn't know
   of values.
9. What a texture works out once from its fields (a placement's axes, a gradient's direction): find each, and give
   CSolidTexture a Changed() the editor calls, so a field changed from outside is as one set at Init.
   Done: CSolidTexture::Changed, virtual.  Three things are worked out from fields: a placement's axes (units at right
   angles), a slope's direction (a unit) and a ramp's keys (in order of their values).  Nothing else is.
10. CSculptNode and the interface over the solid textures; Material Output; the split window with a made material
    (marble) shown as nodes, laid out left to right by how far each is from the output.
    Done.  The node isn't CSculptNode alone: SculptLib has CSculptGraph (the material as nodes: a CSculptNode for the
    output and for each texture, a texture's inputs found from its Class()'s pointers, links made and refused, values
    given), CSculptNodes (the graph in a CNodeEditor, and its listener) and SolidShow (the table of what Class()
    doesn't say: a class's title and kind, a field's label, range and enum names, and which pointers aren't inputs).
    The page is a split, the view over the editor, and its material is marble, baked onto the spheres.  A link changed
    in the editor bakes again.  Nodes not yet placed are arranged in columns by depth, the output on the right, and
    framed when the editor is first laid out.
11. A node's rows from Class(): numbers, ticks, vectors; a change calls Changed() and bakes.
    Done: CSculptField is one number of a texture (a field its Class() names, or one float of a vector) and makes the
    component it's shown with, a number field or a tick; the component's change is put in the texture, the texture is
    told (Changed) and the material baked.  Integers of any size and bools go through the one pair of Get and Set.
12. Measure a bake of each material on each shape; decide the coarse density and the pause from the numbers, and build
    coarse now, whole when still.
    Done.  Measured (TestSculptLib with SCULPT_BAKE_REPORT, Release x64, this machine): a sphere of radius 1 meshed,
    unwrapped and baked, in milliseconds, at 128, 64, 32 and 16 texels a unit:
      Marble   627  161  43  12        Rust     412  105  29   9
      Wood     579  147  39  11        Bricks   276   69  17   6
      Stone    946  247  64  18        Tiles    264   68  18   6
    All but a few milliseconds of each is the bake: meshing and unwrapping again cost next to nothing, so nothing is
    gained by keeping the mesh between bakes.  A quarter of the density is a sixteenth of the time, as expected: 17
    to 64 ms, which follows a drag.  So a change is baked at once at a quarter of the density (32 texels a unit) and
    at the whole (128) a third of a second after the last change (CSculptPage::Rebake, the window's PostAfter).
13. Enums as combos; the table of enums and ranges.
    Done: an enum is a combo box of its names from SolidShow's table, which already had the ranges.  It found a bug
    in StandardLib, fixed there (question 3): an enum field was registered 16 bytes long, the size of an enum written
    with its type, though it's 4 in the object, so saving an object read 12 bytes past the field and loading one wrote
    12 bytes past it, off the end of the object when the enum was its last field (a CSolidMerge).  U_Enum passes the
    enum's size now.
14. Colours: the swatch and the picker.
    Done: WindowLib's CColourField (a named swatch) opens a CColourPicker under itself as a popup: number fields for
    red, green, blue, hue, saturation and value, and a hex field; a grey keeps the hue last chosen.  SupportLib has
    ColourRGBToHSV and back.  In Sculpt it's what an input's own value is shown with (CSculptValue): the input's flat
    texture's colour, or for a factor (a ramp's, a mask's, a warp's amount) a number from 0 to 1 which is a grey.
15. The ramp bar (CColourRampBar) on CSolidRamp's node.
    Done: WindowLib's CColourRampBar (the bar of colours, a marker a stop, dragged past one another; + and -; the
    chosen stop's place and colour), and in Sculpt CSculptRamp, which makes a ramp's keys from the bar's stops.  A key
    is more than a colour (a height, shine, gloss and glow), which the bar knows nothing of: each key keeps the rest
    of its sample wherever its stop is dragged, a stop added is the ramp's whole sample there, and the chosen key's
    four other numbers are fields under the bar.
16. Adding (Shift+A and the Add menu), removing, copying.
    Done.  SolidShow's table says how a new texture of each class is made, for the Add menu (the app's menu bar has
    one, and the editor's own opens under the pointer at Shift+A or the right button: the four kinds, the classes of
    each under them) and for a copy (SolidCopy: the class's new texture given the old one's plain fields, its inputs
    and, of a ramp, its keys).  A cavity is of a shape a node can't give it, so it's shown and neither added nor
    copied.  After a node is added, copied or removed the editor's boxes are made again from the graph (Rebuild),
    which keeps where they were.  Adding a node bakes nothing: the material is the same until it's linked in.
17. CSolidChannel and CSolidCombine (Blender's Separate and Combine).
    Done (ProceduralLib): CSolidChannel is one of a sample's eight numbers as a grey; CSolidCombine is a sample of
    its colour input's colour and alpha and, for its height, shine, gloss and glow, the brightness of an input each.
    Unlinked, a Combine's four are numbers on its node.  In the Add menu as Separate and Combine, under Converter.
18. Undo and redo.
    Done, by keeping the graph: CSculptGraph::Copy is another graph of other textures as this one's, and CSculptNodes
    keeps one after every change.  Undo shows a copy of the one before (so the graph is then another object), redo the
    one after, and a change after an undo forgets what was undone.  A setting dragged changes many times a second:
    settings changed within half a second of each other are one step.  Control+Z and Control+Shift+Z in the editor,
    and Undo and Redo in the app's Edit menu.  Not undone: dropping and clearing spheres, which aren't the graph's.
    The tests' window turns off the objects' validation at every change, as the app does: with a graph kept for each
    change there are too many objects for it.
Around it
19. The shape and the bake's settings (samples, relief, the light) in a panel; what's shown (baked, one channel as
    greys, the normals).
    Done: CSculptPanel, to the right of the view and the editor.  The shape starts as a sphere, so Sculpt opens showing
    marble on one; the spheres dropped by clicking are the Sculpted shape, and clicks drop nothing on the others.  A
    channel is shown by baking a CSolidChannel of the material; the colour alone is the material with no relief.
    Not done: showing the normals.  The bake can make a normal image, but neither renderer draws one, and showing it
    as colours means baking normals where the colours go, which the bake has no way to be asked for.
20. The materials as a menu: New from Marble, Wood, Stone, Rust, Bricks, Tiles, Weathered, each made by its function
    and laid out.
    Done: File's New has the six (SculptMaterials.h), each made by its function and arranged.  Weathered isn't a
    seventh: it's a material round another on a shape, so it's File's Weather, which weathers the material there is on
    the shape shown.  A weathered material has Cavity nodes, of that shape.
21. File's New, Open, Save, Save As: the one file.
    Done.  A Sculpt file is a CSculptDocument (the graph with its nodes where they were, the spheres dropped, the
    panel's settings) written with StandardLib's new WriteNamedObjectFile: a chunk file of the objects, named DRG as
    every object file is.  File has Open, Save, Save As and Export Material, each through a file chooser from the
    window's tick, and Sculpt opens a file given on its command line.  New stays the menu of materials.  Not kept:
    the view (where it's turned to), and the history for undo.  Opening a file isn't undone.
22. A material as a configuration other programs use: the saved graph without Sculpt's node positions, read by one
    call that gives its top texture.  SolidMaterials' functions stay, as the code way of making the same thing.
    Done (ProceduralLib's SolidMaterialFile.h): WriteSolidMaterial writes the graph a texture is the top of to a file
    and ReadSolidMaterial reads one back, which is all another program calls.  A material's file holds only what its
    surface is made of: nodes left unlinked in Sculpt aren't in it.  Sculpt's Export Material writes one, and its Open
    reads one as well as its own files.  SolidMaterials' functions are as they were.
23. Tune the seven materials in Sculpt; the gallery again.
    Not done as written: the materials' numbers are as they were.  Tuning them is by eye and by hand in Sculpt on a
    screen, which is the user's to do (question 4).  What was done: each of the six was opened in Sculpt on the sphere
    and box and looked at (the tests save a Sculpt file of each with SCULPT_MATERIAL_FILES=folder, and HCP65SculptRef
    opens one and saves what its window shows).  Looking found two things, both mended.  The materials are made with
    z up and the view turns about y, so everything was seen from above: what's looked at is now stood up in the view.
    And a box whose top lay where two courses of bricks meet was all mortar: the boxes' tops and bottoms are kept off
    the tenths of a unit.  The gallery is as it was, as no material changed.
24. Clean up; the plan's Done notes.
    Done.  Every step above has its note.  Tidied at the end: the page's description, and a Remesh that had come to do
    nothing but bake.  What isn't done or isn't known, all in one place:
      The node editor doesn't zoom (question 2), and the normals aren't shown (step 19).
      The materials aren't tuned (question 4).
      A Sculpt file doesn't keep the view or the history for undo; opening a file, and dropping spheres, aren't undone.
      Sculpt has been used with a real pointer and keys only a little (question 1: menus, number drags, link drags
      and the colour picker, in Xephyr).  Shift held with a click, and the file choosers of the app itself, are
      untested by hand.
      None of it has been built with Visual Studio.  The projects and solutions are there (HCP65Sculpt.sln,
      TestSculptLib.sln, TestSceneViewLib.sln, and the new files in the libraries' projects), made from the existing
      ones.

Outstanding (2026-11-17, when the work was left for something else; pick up from here)
  1. The bake was slow, and it was what made Sculpt feel slow (question 5).  Measured on the starting marble, a
     1080 by 400 view, a GTX 1050 Ti and 24 cores: every change to a material baked coarsely for about 100 ms on the
     window's thread (about 9 changes a second while a number is dragged), and 330 ms after the last change the whole
     bake held the window for about 1.7 s.  Both were CMeshBake sampling the solid texture (the noises) on one thread.
     Done 2026-11-17 (step 17 of CommonLibraries' StandardLib/ThreadSafetyPlan.txt, which says how and why it is
     safe): the bake's rows are shared among a pool of threads, one for each the hardware runs at once, which the
     app owns (CSculptPage::SetJobs).  The image is the same byte for byte.  In the app, with the machine busy with
     other work: a change's coarse bake 124 ms to 13 ms, the whole bake 2.3 s to 0.18 s.  TestSculptLib's
     SCULPT_BAKE_REPORT prints every material's times on one thread and on the pool.
     A material's texture (TextureSample) and a shape's Distance must not copy, assign or let go of a Ptr: the
     pool's threads aren't registered to.  A Debug build says when one does, and TestSculptBakeJobs bakes every
     starting material that way.
     Left: the whole bake still holds the window while it runs (now under a fifth of a second); if that shows, it's
     run behind the window and shown when it's done.
     Already mended before: the node editor painted its whole back at every layout of the window (half the time of
     turning the view; now only when a link's ends or its size change), Vulkan's frame was read back from slow memory
     (8.4 ms to 0.4 ms), and a coarse bake is one sample a texel.  Turning the view is about 10 ms a frame.
  2. The status line's "ms a frame" is an average smoothed over many frames with the bakes' frames in it, so it reads
     high and isn't the time to turn the view.  Show the last frame's time, or leave the bakes out.
  3. The materials aren't tuned (step 23, question 4): the user's to try in Sculpt.  What they send or name is made
     the starting materials in SolidMaterials.cpp, and the gallery made again.
  4. The normals aren't shown in the view (step 19).
  5. A Sculpt file doesn't keep the view; opening a file and dropping spheres aren't undone.
  6. By hand, not yet tried: Shift held with a click, Open, Save, Save As and Export Material's file choosers.
  7. Build with Visual Studio: HCP65Sculpt.sln, TestSculptLib.sln, TestSceneViewLib.sln.
  8. The node editor's zoom: not wanted for now (question 2).
  9. Nothing is pushed: CommonLibraries, HCP65-Sculpt and WindowInputTester have commits of this work to push when
     the user says.

Questions for the user (kept as they come up; work carries on with the answer assumed in brackets)
  1. Sculpt can only be tried by hand on a screen.  Tests draw it into images with no screen (WinRefLib), which is how
     it's checked as it's built.  May a Xephyr window be opened on your desktop to drive it with a real pointer, as was
     done for the IDE's docking?  [Answered 2026-11-17: yes.  Tried: menus, number drags, link drags and the colour
     picker work with a real pointer and keys.  It found two faults, both mended: Control+Z did nothing (a real
     Control+Z comes as the window's undo command, not as a Z key, which is what was listened for), and with Vulkan a
     whole bake whose size wasn't a power of two lost the device, so the view kept its last picture (a GPU halves mip
     levels rounding down; ours were rounded up: CTextureLevels' bRoundDown).]
  2. The node editor has no zoom.  Blender's wheel zooms it; here a node's settings are real WindowLib components
     (number fields and so on), which can only be drawn at their one size.  Zoom would mean fonts at several sizes and
