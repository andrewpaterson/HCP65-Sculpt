HCP65-Sculpt: the plan
======================
A program for looking at and changing procedural materials on a shape: a 3D view over a node editor, as Blender's Shading
workspace.  It began as "a textures tab" at the end of the texture generators' plan
(CommonLibraries/Libraries/ProceduralLib/TexturePlan.txt, whose steps 1 to 38 made what it shows) and was moved here
2026-11-17.  Proposed, nothing built.  What is decided and what is only proposed is said at each point.


The user's decisions (2026-11-17)
  Looking at textures is not a tab in WindowInputTester.  It's its own project, HCP65-Sculpt, in its own repository
  (Codaphela/HCP65-Sculpt, github andrewpaterson/HCP65-Sculpt; licence files only so far), made with WindowInputTester
  as its inspiration.
  The isosurface tab comes out of WindowInputTester.
  The view both programs draw with goes in a new library in CommonLibraries, SceneViewLib.
  SceneViewLib holds CSceneCanvasDraw; CSceneView, a canvas with the view's mouse and keys and a hook for a click that
  wasn't a drag, which the tester's scene tab and Sculpt both use; and the turntable (target, yaw, pitch, distance,
  framing a scene's bounds, ScreenToPlane) as a plain struct apart from the drawing, so TestSceneViewLib can test it
  without a window.  Not in it: the VRML view (the tester's alone) and the pages.
  The material's graph is edited in a node editor, as Blender's.
  A decision is taken from what Blender does wherever that is unambiguous, and only asked otherwise.  (The user knows
  3D Studio Max, not Blender; Blender is followed as the more used now.)
  No C++ is written out to paste in.  A material is a configuration, data that says what to call with what settings,
  which may be written to disk.  SolidMaterials stays as a help to programmers: functions that make a configuration.
  The configuration is per node: the graph itself, each node naming its operator and holding its settings (not the
  name of a Make function with a seed).

What it's for: the materials' colours and sizes are first guesses, and changing one means changing SolidMaterials.cpp,
building and running the tests for the gallery.  Sculpt is a shape and a material on it, the graph and its numbers
changed and seen at once.  It's where the isosurface tab's dropping of spheres goes too.

The repository, as HCP65-IDE's is laid out:

What comes from WindowInputTester, and how.  Two repositories can't share a source file, so what both want goes to
CommonLibraries and the rest is written again, smaller:
  CSceneCanvasDraw                                              To SceneViewLib (above).
  The app's start (WinMain, the X11 main, the app, its tick, the font)     Written again in Sculpt from the tester's,
      without the tester's pages: some 500 lines.
  CIsosurfacePage                                               Moves to Sculpt and becomes its page.
  The tester keeps its input, editor, scene and VRML tabs, loses File's Isosurface and no longer links ProceduralLib.

Taken from Blender (its Shading workspace and Shader Editor; each is what Blender does, so not asked)
  The window        The 3D view above, the node editor below, a split between; a menu bar on top.
  The 3D view       The middle button's drag turns the view, with Shift it pans, the wheel zooms, Home frames
                    everything.  The left button is for the thing being worked on (here, dropping a sphere), not for
                    the view.  CSceneView's buttons are therefore told to it: the tester's scene tab keeps its own.
  The preview       A material is looked at on a simple shape chosen from a few: sphere, box (Blender's cube), and
                    ours besides: the two met, and what's been sculpted.
  A node            A box with its name in a header, its output socket on the right at the top, its settings in the
                    middle and its input sockets down the left.  Sockets are coloured by what they carry.
  A link            A curve from an output to an input, made by dragging from one socket to the other.  An output
                    feeds any number of inputs; an input takes one link, and a new one replaces it.  Dragging a link
                    off an input and letting go over nothing removes it.
  An input with no link   Uses a value of its own, shown on the node beside the socket as a field (a colour or a
                    number) that goes away when a link is made.
  Every setting shown     All of a node's fields are on the node.  (Blender's way of showing a few named settings is
                    a group of nodes with chosen inputs: later, if wanted.)
  The output        One node, Material Output, is what the shape is given: the graph's top is whatever is linked to
                    it.  Nodes not linked through to it are kept and do nothing.
  Adding            Shift+A, or the Add menu, opens a menu of nodes by kind (Texture, Color, Converter, Vector); the
                    new node is under the pointer.
  Selecting, moving, removing   Click selects, Shift+click adds to the selection, a drag on the background selects in
                    a box, a drag on a node moves the selected ones.  X or Delete removes them, Shift+D copies them.
  Getting about     The middle button's drag pans the editor, the wheel zooms, Home frames all the nodes.
  A number          A field dragged sideways to change it, clicked to type in it; one with a range (0 to 1) shows how
                    far along it is as a bar.
  A ramp            Blender's Color Ramp: a bar of the ramp's colours with its stops as markers to drag, + and - to add
                    and remove a stop, and the chosen stop's place and colour under it.
  A colour          A swatch; clicking it opens a picker with red, green, blue; hue, saturation, value; and hex.
                    Blender's picker also has a wheel: left until wanted.
  Undo              Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, over every change to the graph.
  Names             A node is titled as Blender's where it has the same thing: Noise Texture, Voronoi Texture
                    (CSolidCells), Gradient Texture, Brick Texture, Color Ramp, Mix (CSolidMerge and CSolidMask),
                    Hue/Saturation/Value and Brightness/Contrast (CSolidAdjust).  The classes keep their names.
  Saving            Everything in one file: the shape, the graph, where its nodes are, the view.  File's New, Open,
                    Save, Save As.

Where ours differs from Blender, and what's done about it
  One socket, not several.  Blender's link carries one thing (a colour, a number, a vector) and a texture node has a
  socket for each.  Ours carries a whole sample (colour, height, shine, gloss, glow), and an operator reads the lot.  So
  a node has one output and its links are one colour.  Taking one channel out (Blender's Separate) or putting channels
  from different textures together (Combine) needs two small operators, CSolidChannel and CSolidCombine: step 17.
  Baked, not drawn live.  Blender works a procedural texture out for every pixel on the graphics card as the view is
  drawn.  Ours is baked into the mesh's texels on the processor: half a second to a second for the sphere from the
  texture plan's step 31.  Proposed: on a change bake at a quarter of the density (a sixteenth of the texels, some 50 ms) at once,
  and at the whole density when nothing has changed for a third of a second.  Measured before it's built (step 12).
  An operator's input can be nothing.  The operators were written to be given their inputs.  With links that can be
  taken off, each must do something sensible with none: the input's own value, as Blender's (step 8).
  Where a node is.  CSolidTexture is SupportLib's and knows nothing of editors.  Sculpt keeps a CSculptNode for each
  texture (the texture, where its box is), and saves those.
  Settings come from Class().  Every operator's Class() already names its fields and their kinds (that's how they
  save), so a node's rows are made from it and a new operator needs nothing written in Sculpt.  Two things Class()
  doesn't say are told in a small table in Sculpt, by class and field: which integers are enums (and their names), and
  which numbers have a range.

Where the node editor's code goes.  Proposed: the boxes, sockets, links, selecting, moving, panning and zooming as a
component in WindowLib (CNodeEditor) that knows nothing of textures: it's asked of through an interface (what nodes,
what sockets, may this link be made) which Sculpt implements over the solid textures.  Logicim's circuit editor is the
nearest thing there is (LogicimLib, on SupportLib's CGeometryMap): looked at first, for what can be shared.
The draggable number (CNumberField), the ramp (CColourRampBar) and the colour picker go in WindowLib too.

Steps
The project
1. SceneViewLib in CommonLibraries: CSceneCanvasDraw moved from WindowInputTester (vcxproj, the tester's CMake and
   solution); the tester builds and runs as it did.  Then the turntable split out as a struct, CSceneView made (its
   buttons told to it) and the tester's scene tab put on it, and TestSceneViewLib (the turntable, ScreenToPlane, a
   small scene drawn by the rasteriser; Vulkan needs a device and isn't tested).
2. HCP65-Sculpt's project: the solution, the project, CMake; an app that opens an empty window with a menu (File:
   Exit).  repos.py.
3. CIsosurfacePage moved to Sculpt as its page (CSculptPage) with Blender's buttons: spheres dropped and baked with
   the checker, as now.
4. The isosurface out of WindowInputTester: the page, File's Isosurface, ProceduralLib from its CMake, project and
   solution.
The node editor (WindowLib, with tests at each step)
5. CNumberField: dragged, typed in, a range's bar, a bad number put back.
6. CNodeEditor: nodes as boxes with a header and sockets from the interface; pan, zoom, Home.
7. Links drawn; selecting, box selecting, moving; making, replacing and removing links by dragging.
The graph in Sculpt
8. Operators with an input missing: each input's own value (ProceduralLib; tests).
9. What a texture works out once from its fields (a placement's axes, a gradient's direction): find each, and give
   CSolidTexture a Changed() the editor calls, so a field changed from outside is as one set at Init.
10. CSculptNode and the interface over the solid textures; Material Output; the split window with a made material
    (marble) shown as nodes, laid out left to right by how far each is from the output.
11. A node's rows from Class(): numbers, ticks, vectors; a change calls Changed() and bakes.
12. Measure a bake of each material on each shape; decide the coarse density and the pause from the numbers, and build
    coarse now, whole when still.
13. Enums as combos; the table of enums and ranges.
14. Colours: the swatch and the picker.
15. The ramp bar (CColourRampBar) on CSolidRamp's node.
16. Adding (Shift+A and the Add menu), removing, copying.
17. CSolidChannel and CSolidCombine (Blender's Separate and Combine).
18. Undo and redo.
Around it
19. The shape and the bake's settings (samples, relief, the light) in a panel; what's shown (baked, one channel as
    greys, the normals).
20. The materials as a menu: New from Marble, Wood, Stone, Rust, Bricks, Tiles, Weathered, each made by its function
    and laid out.
21. File's New, Open, Save, Save As: the one file.
22. A material as a configuration other programs use: the saved graph without Sculpt's node positions, read by one
    call that gives its top texture.  SolidMaterials' functions stay, as the code way of making the same thing.
23. Tune the seven materials in Sculpt; the gallery again.
24. Clean up; the plan's Done notes.

Not in it: groups of nodes, frames and reroutes, a colour wheel, muting and collapsing nodes, and any sculpting beyond
dropping spheres.
